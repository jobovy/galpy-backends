**Speed of the native 1D inverse action-angle map.** The test case is `actionAngleVerticalInverse` for an isothermal disk (amp $=1$, $\sigma=0.5$), with energies on linspace(0.05, 2, $n_E$), setup_interp=True and float64 throughout. CPU series are single-threaded (Xeon E5-2697 v4) on a heavily shared machine, where multi-threaded BLAS and torch thrash. GPU series are on an NVIDIA TITAN Xp. (a) Wall time of `xvFreqs(J=0.5, angles)` for $N$ angles on one torus. Dash-dot curves evaluate $N/16$ tori at 16 angles each, with `jax.jit(jax.vmap(...))` over the action or with a Python loop on numpy. Jit compile time ($\approx1.5$–$2$ s at small $N$) is excluded. On the CPU, numpy is fastest at every $N$ (2.1 s at $N=10^5$; torch 5.1 s, eager jax 16.5 s, jitted jax 30 s). The backend path inverts the angle relation with a fixed-schedule bisection: 43 full kernel passes eagerly and 100 under jit, because the traced bracket hides its width. numpy instead uses a safeguarded Newton iteration that needs a handful of passes. As a result, jit beats eager only at small $N$ (6.5 ms vs 0.25 s at $N=10$), where eager jax is limited by per-operation dispatch. (b) Construction time against $n_E$. Filled markers are warm calls and open markers are first calls: eager jax recompiles every operation for each new shape ($\approx9$–$11$ s), and jit traces and compiles ($\approx3$–$5$ s). Warm native construction is faster than numpy's per-torus scipy fits. At $n_E=256$, torch takes 0.53 s, eager jax 0.65 s and jitted jax 1.4 s, against 4.7 s for numpy. The backend construction is what makes the family differentiable in the potential's parameters (Fig. gV1). Its evaluation cost is a known, fixable inefficiency of the generic root finder (BUG_vi_backend_eval_bisection.md), not something inherent to the map.
