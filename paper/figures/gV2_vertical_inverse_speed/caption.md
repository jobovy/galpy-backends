**Speed of the native 1D inverse action-angle map.** The test case is `actionAngleVerticalInverse` for an isothermal disk (amp $=1$, $\sigma=0.5$), with energies on linspace(0.05, 2, $n_E$), setup_interp=True and float64 throughout. Every CPU series ran on one core of an otherwise idle Xeon E5-2697 v4 (re-measured 2026-10-08 pinned with `taskset`: numpy and torch are single-threaded, and pinning is the only way to keep jax's XLA:CPU thread pool to one core). GPU series are on an NVIDIA TITAN Xp (jax 0.10.1, torch 2.12.0+cu126) and, re-measured, on an NVIDIA H100 80GB (Trillium; jax 0.11.2, torch 2.14.1+cu130). Colour encodes framework and device (numpy black; jax sky blue/blue/navy and torch orange/vermillion/dark red for CPU/TITAN Xp/H100); jitted series are solid, eager ones dashed; lines are labelled directly. (a) Wall time of `xvFreqs(J=0.5, angles)` for $N$ angles on one torus. Dash-dot curves ("vmap", "loop") evaluate $N/16$ tori at 16 angles each, with `jax.jit(jax.vmap(...))` over the action or with a Python loop on numpy. Jit compile time ($\approx1.1$–$2$ s on the CPU, $\approx1.1$–$1.8$ s on the TITAN Xp) is excluded. The backend inverts the angle relation with the same safeguarded Newton iteration as numpy, started at the requested angle. On one CPU core numpy is the fastest at every $N$: jitted jax ties it at $N=10$ (0.75 vs 0.76 ms; eager torch 16 ms, eager jax 0.13 s, limited by per-operation dispatch) and is 2–8× slower from $N=30$ on, taking 47 s at $N=10^6$ against 9.8 s for numpy, 50 s for eager torch and 64 s for eager jax. Jitted jax is faster than eager jax at every $N$. For many tori, the jitted vmap is 1.4–1.8× faster than the numpy loop over tori at every $N$ (36 s vs 54 s at $N=10^6$, $\approx0.9$ ms per torus for numpy) and even cheaper per point than the one-torus jit. On the TITAN Xp, jitted jax stays at a 0.6 ms launch floor up to $N\approx300$ and takes 0.31 s at $N=10^6$; eager torch has a 0.1 s floor and takes 0.94 s. On the H100, jitted jax stays at 0.26 ms up to $N\approx3000$ and takes 11 ms at $N=10^6$; eager torch takes 70 ms. On both GPUs the vmap over tori costs nearly the same as the one-torus jit. (b) Construction time against $n_E$. Filled markers are warm calls and open markers (thin dotted) are first calls: eager jax compiles every operation for each new shape ($\approx5$–$6$ s on the CPU, $\approx8$–$10$ s on the TITAN Xp), and jit traces and compiles ($\approx2.4$–$3.3$ s on the CPU). Warm torch and jitted construction are faster than numpy's per-torus scipy fits at every $n_E$, and eager jax from $n_E=16$ on. At $n_E=256$, torch takes 0.25 s, eager jax 0.45 s and jitted jax 0.77 s, against 2.4 s for numpy. Warm jitted construction takes 9–54 ms on the TITAN Xp and 3.6–5.8 ms on the H100 over the whole $n_E$ range. The backend construction is what makes the family differentiable in the potential's parameters (Fig. gV1).
