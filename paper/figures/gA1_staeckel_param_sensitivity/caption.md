**Sensitivity of Staeckel-approximation actions to the flattening of the dark-matter halo, by automatic differentiation through galpy's pure-Python action-angle code.**
For a Sun-like orbit ($R=1$, $v_R=0.1$, $v_T=1.1$, $z=0.1$, $v_z=0.05$ in natural units) in `LogarithmicHaloPotential(normalize=1, q)`, with `actionAngleStaeckel(delta=0.45, c=False)` rebuilt from $q$ inside the differentiated function, panel (a) shows the radial and vertical actions $J_R$ and $J_z$ over $0.7\le q\le1.1$, and panel (b) their derivatives $\partial J/\partial q$ from `jax.grad` (lines) together with central finite differences of the numpy path at $h=10^{-5}$ (open circles), which agree to better than $2.3\times10^{-9}$ (relative) at every marked point.
Panel (c) shows, at $q=0.9$, the relative difference between the autodiff derivative and central finite differences as a function of the step $h$, with the potential and the action-angle object rebuilt at $q\pm h$: it follows the $h^2$ truncation error down to a floor of $1.1\times10^{-10}$ ($\partial J_R/\partial q$, $h=10^{-6}$) and $1.3\times10^{-10}$ ($\partial J_z/\partial q$, $h=10^{-5}$), below which floating-point round-off in the finite difference takes over.
The autodiff value therefore sits at the bottom of the finite-difference V, i.e., it is exact to the precision with which finite differences can test it, and it comes from a single reverse pass (0.4–1.2 ms per gradient once the user `jax.jit`s it) rather than two extra action evaluations per parameter.
All computations are in float64 on the CPU.
