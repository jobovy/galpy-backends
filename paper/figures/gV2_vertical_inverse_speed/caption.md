**Speed of the native 1D inverse action-angle map.** The test case is `actionAngleVerticalInverse` for an isothermal disk (amp $=1$, $\sigma=0.5$), with energies on linspace(0.05, 2, $n_E$), setup_interp=True and float64 throughout. CPU series ran on an otherwise idle Xeon E5-2697 v4 (72 hardware threads); numpy and torch used one thread each, while jax's XLA:CPU could use all 72 (jax 0.10.1 ignores the single-thread XLA flags the measurement set), so the jax CPU curves are not single-core numbers. GPU series are on an NVIDIA TITAN Xp (jax 0.10.1, torch 2.12.0+cu126) and, re-measured, on an NVIDIA H100 80GB (Trillium; jax 0.11.2, torch 2.14.1+cu130). Colour encodes framework and device (numpy black; jax sky blue/blue/navy and torch orange/vermillion/dark red for CPU/TITAN Xp/H100); jitted series are solid, eager ones dashed; lines are labelled directly. (a) Wall time of `xvFreqs(J=0.5, angles)` for $N$ angles on one torus. Dash-dot curves ("vmap", "loop") evaluate $N/16$ tori at 16 angles each, with `jax.jit(jax.vmap(...))` over the action or with a Python loop on numpy. Jit compile time ($\approx0.6$–$2.6$ s on the CPU, $\approx1.1$–$1.8$ s on the TITAN Xp) is excluded. The backend inverts the angle relation with the same safeguarded Newton iteration as numpy, started at the requested angle. On the CPU, numpy is fastest up to $N\approx3000$ (0.8 ms at $N=10$, against 2.3 ms for jitted jax, 22 ms for eager torch and 0.20 s for eager jax, which is limited by per-operation dispatch). Jitted jax (multi-threaded, see above) overtakes numpy at $N\approx10^4$ and is $\approx5\times$ faster at $N=10^6$: 2.0 s, against 9.6 s for numpy, 14 s for eager jax and 51 s for eager torch. Jitted jax is faster than eager jax at every $N$. For many tori, the jitted vmap costs $\approx4.5\times$ the one-torus jit per point on the CPU (9.1 s at $N=10^6$) but still beats the numpy loop over tori ($\approx0.9$ ms per torus; 57 s) from $\approx200$ tori on. On the TITAN Xp, jitted jax stays at a 0.6 ms launch floor up to $N\approx300$ and takes 0.31 s at $N=10^6$; eager torch has a 0.1 s floor and takes 0.94 s. On the H100, jitted jax stays at 0.26 ms up to $N\approx3000$ and takes 11 ms at $N=10^6$; eager torch takes 70 ms. On both GPUs the vmap over tori costs nearly the same as the one-torus jit. (b) Construction time against $n_E$. Filled markers are warm calls and open markers (thin dotted) are first calls: eager jax compiles every operation for each new shape ($\approx11$–$14$ s on the CPU, $\approx8$–$10$ s on the TITAN Xp), and jit traces and compiles ($\approx1.2$–$2.1$ s). Warm torch and jitted construction are faster than numpy's per-torus scipy fits at every $n_E$, and eager jax from $n_E=16$ on. At $n_E=256$, torch takes 0.25 s, eager jax 0.36 s and jitted jax 0.48 s, against 2.5 s for numpy. Warm jitted construction takes 9–54 ms on the TITAN Xp and 3.6–5.8 ms on the H100 over the whole $n_E$ range. The backend construction is what makes the family differentiable in the potential's parameters (Fig. gV1).
