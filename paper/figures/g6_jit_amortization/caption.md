**Figure G6 — compile-once / run-many amortization (jax and torch; CPU, TITAN Xp, H100; float64).**

A compiled backend trades a one-time compilation for fast steady-state execution. Measured for a vectorized Plummer $F_R$ at $N=10^6$ points on one CPU core (jax), the development box's NVIDIA TITAN Xp (jax 0.10.1, torch 2.12.0+cu126) and an NVIDIA H100 80GB on the Trillium cluster (jax 0.11.2, torch 2.14.1+cu130; darker colours). Compiled calls are solid lines, eager calls dashed.

*(a)* Wall-time per call vs call index for a freshly compiled function. **Call 0 includes compilation** — jax: $\sim\!100$ ms on the CPU, $\sim\!130$ ms on the TITAN Xp, $\sim\!60$ ms on the H100; torch.compile on the H100: $0.57$ s (and $7.8$ s for the first compilation in the process) — after which every call runs the cached executable at the steady-state cost (jax: $26$ ms on the CPU, $0.86$ ms on the TITAN Xp, $0.048$ ms on the H100; torch compiled on the H100: the same $0.048$ ms, so the two H100 lines coincide and share one label). The dashed lines mark the eager per-call time: jax $98$ ms on the CPU, $3.2$ ms on the TITAN Xp, $0.72$ ms on the H100; torch $0.98$ ms on the TITAN Xp and $0.10$ ms on the H100. torch.compile's inductor backend refuses the TITAN Xp (Triton needs compute capability $\geq 7.0$, the card is 6.1), so torch appears there eagerly only.

*(b)* Cumulative time vs number of calls, in units of each device's eager call (so all devices share one axis; the eager diagonal is the same for every device), log–log. A compiled line starts at its compile cost and rises at its steady cost per call; it overtakes the eager diagonal at the **break-even** (dots; the count is in each line's label): $\approx 2$ calls on the CPU (eager jax is slowest there), $\approx 58$ on the TITAN Xp and $\approx 94$ on the H100 for jax — later on the faster card because its eager calls are already cheap while the compile cost only halves — and $\approx 10^4$ for torch.compile on the H100, whose eager call is already within a factor of two of the compiled one.

**Take-away:** compilation is a fixed up-front cost amortized over calls; for any workload that evaluates the same shape repeatedly — an orbit integration makes millions of force calls — jit is far past break-even. Eager torch dispatches one kernel per operation with little overhead, so for an operation this simple it is nearly as fast as the fused kernel; the gain of torch.compile is in fusing longer computations.

Data: `data/g6_jit_amortization.json` (TITAN Xp, jax), `data/cpu/` (one core, jax; `measure_cpu.py`), `data/h100/` (H100, jax), `data/torch/` (TITAN Xp, torch eager + the compile error; `measure_torch.py`), `data/h100/torch/` (H100, torch; `measure_torch.py --out`, run by the backends-h100 session on 2026-10-28, galpy feat/backends 34bca2d1d).
