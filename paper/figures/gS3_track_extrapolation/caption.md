**Figure GS3: one forward-mode AD pass predicts a spray stream's smooth track for large changes in the halo's flattening.**
A `fardal15spraydf` stream with both tails (N = 6000 particles, 3000 per tail) is integrated for 4.5 Gyr in a logarithmic halo with q₀ = 0.9; the progenitor orbit is inclined, so the track probes q. A single `jax.jvp` pass gives every particle's tangent d(x, v)/dq, and moving the particles linearly by Δq predicts the stream at q₀ + Δq. The direct re-runs integrate the full spray at the new q with the same random key. Everything is shown in one stream-aligned frame (φ₁ along the progenitor's orbital plane, φ₂ across it), built with `Orbit.align_to_orbit()` from the q-independent initial condition and passed as `custom_sky_transform`.

**(a)** The smooth tracks, fitted with `streamTrack` (default GCV smoothing) to the directly re-run particles (solid) and to the AD-predicted particles (dashed), for q = 0.7–1.1.

**(b)** The across-track shift being predicted (RMS Δφ₂ between the track at q₀ + Δq and at q₀) and the residual after one AD pass (RMS Δφ₂ between the AD-predicted and the direct track), against |Δq|; the worse of ±Δq is shown. For a like-for-like comparison everything except the particles is held fixed: the random key, the frame, the φ₁ grid (the inner 5–95% of each tail at q₀) and the smoother (a Gaussian-kernel mean of φ₂ at each grid φ₁ with a fixed 2° bandwidth). The residual then falls as Δq² (dotted guide) from |Δq| = 0.2 down to 0.003, so the removed fraction rises towards 100%: one pass removes 67% of the shift at |Δq| = 0.2, 92% at 0.025 and 99% at 0.003. The remaining residual is the genuine second-order response: over 4.5 Gyr a change in q shifts the particles' orbital phases, so they slide along the curved stream, and the per-particle φ₂ residual already reaches 28% of the per-particle shift at |Δq| = 0.025 (it too scales as Δq²). The grey crosses compare two independent `streamTrack` fits instead. Their residual stops falling as Δq² below |Δq| ≈ 0.025 and levels off at 5–18% of the shift, because the fit is by design not a smooth function of the particles (hard bins in the progenitor time, a closest-point assignment on a discrete orbit grid, a data-dependent range): particles that the O(Δq²) difference moves across a bin edge make finite jumps whose random sum scales as Δq. Binning the same particles in fixed hard φ₁ bins (30 or 60 per tail) gives the same plateau, 82–93% removed at every |Δq| ≤ 0.025, which is what limited an earlier version of this figure to 85% at |Δq| = 0.025.
