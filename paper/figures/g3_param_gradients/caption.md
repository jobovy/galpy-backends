**Figure G3 — Autodiff with respect to potential parameters (CPU, float64).**

The complement to G1: where G1 differentiates w.r.t. the *coordinates* (the identity $\partial\Phi/\partial R = -F_R$), here the differentiation is w.r.t. the potential's *constructor parameters* (amplitude, scale length) — the quantities an inference or optimization problem varies. This is enabled by galpy's unit-parser now passing backend arrays (jax/torch, including traced ones) through construction unscaled (`galpy.backend.is_backend_array`).

*(a) Correctness.* For Plummer and Isochrone, the parameter gradient of the potential $\Phi$ and the radial force $F_R$ w.r.t. the amplitude and the scale length $b$. Two independent checks per quantity: **jax vs torch** (filled markers) — the two autodiff engines agree to $\sim\!10^{-16}$ (below float64 $\epsilon$), confirming both compute the *exact* derivative; and **autodiff vs central finite-difference** (open markers) — agreement at $\sim\!10^{-10}$, set by the finite-difference accuracy floor, not by the autodiff (which is exact to round-off). The FD curve is the noisy one.

*(b) A worked example.* Recovering a Plummer scale length by gradient descent: starting from $b=1.5$, the parameter is fit to a target $F_R(R)$ profile (generated with $b_{\rm true}=0.85$) by minimizing $\langle (F_R(b)-F_R^{\rm target})^2\rangle$, with the gradient $\mathrm{d}\,\mathrm{loss}/\mathrm{d}b$ supplied by autodiff. Both `jax.grad` and `torch.autograd` drive the identical descent (the curves overlie) monotonically to the true value, recovering $b=0.850000$ and a loss below $10^{-17}$. The inset shows the parameter trajectory converging to $b_{\rm true}$.

**Take-away:** galpy potentials are differentiable w.r.t. their parameters under both backends, exactly (to round-off) and identically — the prerequisite for gradient-based fitting of potential models. Usage note: the coordinates are supplied as backend arrays too, so the namespace resolver follows the data into jax/torch.
