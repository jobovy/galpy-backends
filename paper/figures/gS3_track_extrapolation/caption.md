**Figure GS3: one forward-mode AD pass predicts a spray stream's smooth track for large changes in the halo's flattening.**
A `fardal15spraydf` stream of N = 1500 particles is integrated for 4.5 Gyr in a logarithmic halo with q₀ = 0.9; the progenitor orbit is inclined, so the track probes q. A single `jax.jvp` pass gives every particle's tangent d(x, v)/dq. Moving the particles linearly by Δq, and fitting the smooth track with the same `streamTrack` machinery, predicts the track at q₀ + Δq (dashed). The solid lines are the direct re-runs: the full spray at the new q, with the same random key. All tracks are shown in one stream-aligned frame (φ₁ along the progenitor's orbital plane, φ₂ across it), built with `Orbit.align_to_orbit()` from the q-independent initial condition and passed as `custom_sky_transform`.

**(a)** The tracks for q = 0.7–1.1, with the one-pass AD predictions dashed.

**(b)** The across-track shift being predicted (the RMS Δφ₂ between the direct track and the q₀ track, at fixed φ₁ over their common range) against the residual after one AD pass, as a function of |Δq|. A single pass removes 85% of the shift at |Δq| = 0.025 and 62% at |Δq| = 0.2. The residual grows roughly linearly with Δq rather than quadratically: the per-particle tangents are exact (they agree with a central finite difference to 1.6e-5), so part of the residual comes from comparing two independently fitted smooth tracks, not from the derivative. The RMS is used because the maximum over φ₁ is dominated by the sparse far ends of the tails, where any two spline fits scatter.
