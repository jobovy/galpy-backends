**Figure G6 — jit compile-once / run-many amortization (jax, GPU, float64).**

A jit-compiled backend trades a one-time XLA compilation for fast steady-state execution. Measured for a vectorized Plummer $F_R$ on two GPUs: the development box's NVIDIA TITAN Xp (jax 0.10.1) and an NVIDIA H100 80GB on the Trillium cluster (jax 0.11.2; darker blue). jit-compiled calls are solid lines, eager (un-jit) calls dashed.

*(a)* Wall-time per call vs call index for a freshly-jitted function at two array sizes. **Call 0 includes compilation** — a $\sim\!110$–$120$ ms spike on the TITAN Xp, $\sim\!60$ ms on the H100 — after which every call runs the cached executable at the steady-state cost (TITAN Xp: $\sim\!0.7$ ms at $N=10^6$, $\sim\!0.13$ ms at $N=10^4$; H100: $\sim\!0.07$ and $\sim\!0.05$ ms). The dashed lines mark the eager (un-jit) per-call time ($\sim\!2.2$–$3$ ms on the TITAN Xp, $\sim\!0.7$ ms on the H100, dispatch-bound and nearly $N$-independent): once compiled, jit is several times faster per call, and much more so at small $N$ where eager is dominated by Python/dispatch overhead.

*(b)* Cumulative wall-time vs number of calls at $N=10^6$. The jit curve starts a compile-cost behind the eager curve but has a shallower slope (lower per-call cost), so it overtakes eager after a **break-even of $\approx\!71$ calls on the TITAN Xp and $\approx\!102$ on the H100** — later on the faster card because its eager calls are already cheap, while the compile cost only halves.

**Take-away:** compilation is a fixed up-front cost amortized over calls; for any workload that evaluates the same shape repeatedly — an orbit integration makes millions of force calls — jit is far past break-even and the compile time is negligible. The break-even count is the relevant figure of merit for short, one-off evaluations.
