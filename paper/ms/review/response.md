## Part A — Audits behind the corrections

### A.1 Stale "numpy only" statements

Several claims of the draft described the state of the code in September and were no longer true. Each was tested with an automatic derivative against a central finite difference that rebuilds the object from the perturbed parameter (the test that the differentiability audit of 2026-09-16 established), on jax and, where noted, torch and the TITAN Xp:

| claim in the draft | measured on `feat/backends` | comment |
|---|---|---|
| 49 of 50 potentials differentiable w.r.t. amplitude under compilation; DiskSCF fails under `jax.jit` | all 47 potentials that take only numerical parameters (King: its mass) differentiate eagerly and under `jax.jit`, with identical values; DiskSCF's density-parameter derivative through the coefficients: rel. 1e-11 | [58], [59] |
| the adiabatic-contraction wrapper "declares itself incompatible and is computed on numpy" | d/df_bar and d/da of the contracted NFW halo: rel. 3e-11, 2e-11 | [56] |
| interpolated potentials "not differentiable in the tabulated values" | interpRZPotential d/da: 1e-11; SCF/Multipole/DiskSCF/DiskMultipole from a density: 1e-9–1e-11; interpSphericalPotential from a *function*: yes; from a *galpy potential*: **failed** → PR #1764 | [60] |
| MovingObjectPotential "keeps that orbit on numpy" | with a jax orbit the field is differentiable w.r.t. the host potential's parameter through the orbit (rel. 8e-8, finite-difference limited), under `jax.jit`, and on the TITAN Xp | [61] |

Two further gaps that the audit found and JB had not flagged:

- the two-dimensional disk DFs (`dehnendf`, `shudf`, `schwarzschilddf`) sample by rejection on numpy and take no `key=`, so the draft's "every sampler in galpy takes a `key=` argument" was false; the text now says so (§6.1, §6.3), and these samplers are now inverse-CDF samplers with `key=` (PR #1769, Part B), which also corrected a bias in the old `sample`;

Its kernels are tested under torch; the full construction is not, because of the cost (Part B).

### A.2 numpy byte-identity

(The audit scripts and their README are in `review/byteid_audit/`.)

The draft claimed byte-identity of the numpy path "except in three places, all in the DFs". Measured directly (checksums of Φ, forces, density and second derivatives of every parameter-free potential on a 40×9 grid; orbits, actions and DF values at fixed points; main `9c129abcb` against `feat/backends` `db33e5fcf`, both with freshly built C extensions):

- **identical**: the Eddington, Hernquist, Osipkov–Merritt, constant-anisotropy (which the draft listed as changed; restored by #1752), Dehnen and Shu DFs; `rk4_c` orbits; the spherical and isochrone-approximation actions;
- **deliberately changed**: the King model (its Poisson equation is solved to rtol 1e-10 instead of scipy's default 1e-3, PR #1552: 1e-5 to 1e-3 relative in Φ and the DF), and the particular draws of the quasi-isothermal, disk-DF (#1769) and stream samplers (same distribution, except that the disk-DF `sample` now also samples the DF correctly, #1769);
- **rounding-level** (a few ulp, ≤ 5e-15 relative; ≤ 2e-14 in one second derivative): NFW, Burkert, Einasto, PowerSphericalPotentialwCutoff and the eight ellipsoidal potentials' second derivatives, whose Python formulas were restructured for small-radius accuracy and for differentiability w.r.t. their shape parameters; everything computed from these potentials' Python forces (the python integrators, the Python Stäckel code, `estimateDeltaStaeckel`, the King potential) differs at the same level. The C sources of the potentials are unchanged (`dop853_c` differs by one ulp between the two builds, a compiler artefact). **Now reverted for all but Einasto and the cutoff power law** (below);
- a dozen methods became **array-capable** on `feat/backends` (DehnenBar, KuzminKutuzov, RazorThinExponentialDisk), which is not a numeric change.

§2.1 now states exactly this. JB chose to **restore** byte-identity where feasible rather than document it. Comparing the formulas of the two branches method by method found three operation-order drifts, now reverted (PR #1768, Part B): the NFW small-radius branch (`-log1p(r/a)/r` vs `-(1/r)*log1p`, `t*t/(1+t)` vs `t*u`, the series and the `/r^5` placement), the Burkert small-radius series (a generic power series instead of main's Horner loop in `x^4`), and the ellipsoidal second-derivative loop (`xx = xx + a + b` associates differently from `xx += a + b`). With these, NFW, Burkert and all eight ellipsoidal potentials are bitwise identical to main again, on the default-parameter grid and on a second grid of non-default parameters. Two potentials stay at the rounding level because their force *formulas* changed, and a numpy-only copy of the old formula would be the dual path the branch forbids: Einasto below its scale radius (a 1F1 series whose derivative with respect to the scale radius the closed form loses to cancellation; ≤ 5e-15) and PowerSphericalPotentialwCutoff (the regularized incomplete gamma function instead of `hyp1f1`; ≤ 4e-15, 2e-14 in `R2deriv` at α = 0.5). §2.1 names these two. A test now pins the numpy path to the pre-backend formulas bitwise.
