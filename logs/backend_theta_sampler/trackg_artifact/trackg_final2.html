galpy  ·  differentiable galactic dynamics  ·  Track G

The shape of a stream is now a differentiable function of the Galaxy.

A tidal stellar stream traces a smooth curve through phase space. That curve — and the whole spray of stars it is built from — now carries exact gradients with respect to where the progenitor started and what the dark-matter halo is made of. The entire generative model is differentiable, jit-compiled, and runs on the GPU, so the Milky Way's potential can be fit to a stream by gradient descent rather than by sampling a black box.

One gradient, taken honestly

The stars aren't data — they're a function of the halo.

It is tempting to treat the spray as a fixed point cloud and ask only how the smooth track slides when you turn the potential. But the stars are a theory intermediate: change the halo and every one of them is re-stripped and re-integrated onto a new orbit. The physical gradient ∂ track / ∂ θ has to let the whole stream re-form — freeze the stars and you drop its dominant term.

∂ track / ∂ θ — the halo's mass and flattening. Turning either one re-integrates all 600 sample orbits (and the progenitor) natively in jax / torch through an in-backend ODE (diffrax / torchdiffeq) — the whole spray flows, under jit, on the GPU.

∂ track / ∂ progenitor — the stream's initial conditions. Nudge the progenitor's launch velocity and every star is re-thrown from the shifted orbit; the whole track swings. The fast eager path carries this through galpy's compiled C integrator as a state-transition matrix (C-STM).

Freezing the stars underestimates the physical displacement of the track by these factors — for a radial kick to the progenitor, by 70×. The frozen-star gradient isn't a small approximation; it is the wrong quantity.

The payoff

Fit the dark-matter halo to a stream — by descending its gradient.

Inferring the Milky Way's potential from a stream usually means an expensive sample over a forward model you can only evaluate, never differentiate. With the generative ∂ track / ∂ θ in hand — every star flowing — the same problem becomes ordinary optimization: start from a wrong halo mass and let the gradient walk it home.

Under the hood

How the gradient stays physical through a pipeline full of non-differentiable steps.

A stream track is assembled from operations that look hostile to autodiff — a closest-point assignment, a GCV-smoothed spline, a nearest-positive-definite covariance projection. The trick is to freeze the discrete structure — which star maps where, the smoothing bandwidth, the eigenvectors — as a stop-gradient hyperparameter, while the star positions flow through as differentiable data. Freeze the bookkeeping, not the stars: the assignment is a property of the observed stream, but every star's phase-space coordinate is a live function of the halo.

  • Every orbit on the backend — the progenitor and all 600 spray stars integrated by the in-backend ODE (diffrax/torchdiffeq), jittable and GPU-resident; the eager C-STM (dop853_c) preserved for fast initial-condition gradients.
  • Reparameterized spray — the stripping times and kick velocities are drawn from a backend RNG key, so the noise is common across a gradient step; autodiff and finite difference see the identical realization.
  • Frozen-operator reconstruction — closest-point assignment, GCV bandwidth, and PSD eigenvectors detached; the offset → bin → smooth → covariance map stays a differentiable linear operator in the flowing data.
