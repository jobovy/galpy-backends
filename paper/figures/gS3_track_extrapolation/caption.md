**Figure GS3: the derivative of a spray stream's fitted track predicts the track for large changes in the halo's flattening.**
A `fardal15spraydf` stream with both tails (N = 6000 particles, 3000 per tail) is integrated for 4.5 Gyr in a logarithmic halo with q₀ = 0.9; the progenitor orbit is inclined, so the track probes q. The track is galpy's `streamTrack(n=6000, key=key, tail='both')` (its default pspline fit), i.e. sampling, integration and fit in one function of q; one `jax.jit(jax.jvp)` pass gives the track at q₀ and its derivative d track/dq, and track(q₀) + Δq · d track/dq predicts the track at q₀ + Δq. The direct tracks re-run the whole function at the new q with the same random key. Every track uses the same jitted fit (which equals the eager one), and everything is shown in one stream-aligned frame (φ₁ along the progenitor's orbital plane, φ₂ across it), built with `Orbit.align_to_orbit()` from the q-independent initial condition and passed as `custom_sky_transform`.

**(a)** The tracks at q = 0.7–1.1 computed directly (solid) and their first-order prediction (dashed), with the q₀ track (thick).

**(b)** The across-track shift being predicted (RMS Δφ₂ at fixed φ₁ between the track at q₀ + Δq and at q₀, over the central 80% of each tail) and the residual of the first-order prediction, against |Δq|, for Δq > 0 (filled) and Δq < 0 (open). The prediction removes 80% of the shift at |Δq| = 0.2, 90% at 0.1, 97% at 0.025 and 99% at |Δq| ≤ 0.006 (bottom row, worse of ±Δq). The residual falls as |Δq|^1.9 over the whole range, with no floor. Grey crosses: moving the particles to first order (one `jax.jvp` of `sample`) and re-fitting the track at q₀ + Δq, whose residual is 1.5–2.4 times larger at every step. The derivative agrees with central finite differences of the same jitted track to 3.6 × 10⁻⁴, 2.7 × 10⁻⁵ and 2.4 × 10⁻⁶ for steps of 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵ (smaller steps are limited by the integration's round-off, ~2 × 10⁻⁵).
